45–55 min
Official activity: Complete the collaboration checklist about responsibility, respectful communication, shared work, data care, and persistence while cleaning the assigned dataset. Record at least 3 cleaned values in a change log. Then create a simple model that represents one category, object, or display idea from your data topic.
Dataset context: A school environmental team conducted a one-day waste and recycling collection around the campus. Each row is one collection record showing the school location, the number of individual items collected, and the material category assigned to those items. Different team members entered the data, so the dataset may contain missing information, duplicate records, unreasonable quantities, inconsistent units, or inconsistent category names. Clean the data without inventing information, then use reliable records to compare two collection points.

Built-in assessment support

Review, hints, and data-care notes

Use these general reminders at any point. They explain safe data-cleaning decisions but do not tell you which rows to change.

Review: what to look for

Missing information

If the correct value is unknown, do not guess. Use Not provided.

Unreliable quantity

A negative amount or a very large amount may not be reliable without another source.

Duplicate record

If two complete records are exactly the same, keep the first one and mark the later copy as a duplicate.

Consistent format

Check spelling, capitalization, units, and category names so similar information is written the same way.

Hints: questions to ask yourself

  • What evidence in this row makes me think a change is needed?
  • Do I know the correct value, or would changing it mean I am guessing?
  • Does this row repeat an earlier complete record?
  • Will this correction make the dataset more consistent and easier to compare?

Notes: explain your evidence

Change log: “I changed ___ to ___ because ___.”

Missing value: “I used Not provided because the dataset does not give a reliable value.”

Duplicate: “I removed the later copy because it repeats row ___.”

Peer review: “I explained ___, and my partner suggested ___.”
